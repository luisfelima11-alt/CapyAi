Grammar: Will vs Going To

Two future forms — each with a specific purpose.

Spontaneous: "I'm hungry. I'll order a pizza." (decided right now)
Prediction: "I think it will rain tomorrow." (just an opinion)
Promise: "I promise I'll call you tonight."
Offer: "Don't worry, I'll help you."
Plan already made: "She's going to travel to London — she already booked the flight."
Evidence-based prediction: "Look at those clouds! It's going to rain." (you can see the evidence)
Intention already decided: "I'm going to study abroad — I've already applied."

gonna = going to (spoken/informal only — NEVER use in formal writing!)

"I'm gonna call you later." = "I'm going to call you later."
"She's gonna make it." = "She is going to make it."
🎵 You'll hear "gonna" all the time in songs by Shawn Mendes, Adele, Katy Perry — it's natural spoken English!

Pronunciation Tip: gonna

In natural spoken English and in songs, "going to" becomes "gonna". This is completely natural — but only in informal speech, never in formal writing.

INFORMAL / SPOKEN
"I'm gonna be okay."
FORMAL / WRITTEN
"I am going to be okay."

🎤 You'll hear "gonna" in almost every pop song — by Shawn Mendes, Adele, Katy Perry and One Direction!
