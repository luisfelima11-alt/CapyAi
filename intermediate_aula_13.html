Grammar: Relative Clauses

Pronomes relativos, defining vs non-defining, e quando omitir o pronome.

WHO → people: "The doctor who treated me was excellent."
WHICH → things/animals: "The car which I bought is already broken."
THAT → people OR things (informal, defining clauses only): "The person that called / The book that I read."
WHOSE → possession (people/things): "The artist whose work I admire / The company whose shares crashed."
WHERE → places: "The city where I grew up / The office where she works."
WHEN → time: "The year when I graduated / The moment when everything changed."
DEFINING (no commas): Essential information — removes it and the sentence loses meaning.
"The student who failed the test must retake it." (Only that specific student — not all students.)
NON-DEFINING (with commas): Extra information — remove it and the sentence still makes sense.
"My sister, who lives in London, is a lawyer." (We already know who my sister is — this is extra info.)
KEY RULE: Non-defining clauses NEVER use "that" — always use who/which/whose/where.
❌ "My phone, that I dropped, is broken."  ✅ "My phone, which I dropped, is broken."
You can omit who/which/that when it is the OBJECT of the relative clause:
"The film (that) I watched was great." ← 'that' is object of 'watched' → can omit ✅
"The actor who starred in it was amazing." ← 'who' is subject of 'starred' → CANNOT omit ❌
Quick test: If the relative pronoun is followed immediately by a verb → it's the subject → cannot omit.
If the relative pronoun is followed by a noun/pronoun → it's the object → can omit.
