📐 Grammar

WILL vs GOING TO — a diferença que importa.

A Diferença Essencial

⚡ WILL → decisão espontânea / promessa / previsão sem evidência
📅 GOING TO → plano já decidido / evidência visível no presente
✅ Ambos falam do futuro — mas a escolha muda o significado!

Use WILL para decisões tomadas no momento, promessas e previsões gerais:

💡 Decisão espontânea: "The phone is ringing! — I'll get it!"

🤝 Promessa: "I'll never forget this moment, I promise."

🔮 Previsão: "I think it will rain tomorrow." / "You'll love this place!"

🙋 Oferta: "I'll carry that for you — it looks heavy!"

Use GOING TO para planos decididos e evidências visíveis:

📋 Plano pré-decidido: "I'm going to study tonight — it's in my calendar."

🌩️ Evidência visível: "Look at those clouds — it's going to rain!"

💪 Intenção firme: "She's going to move to London next year — flights are booked!"

Como montar as frases:

WILL: sujeito + will + verbo base → I will go / She will help

Won't = will not → I won't be late, I promise!

GOING TO: sujeito + am/is/are going to + verbo base

I am going to study. / She is going to travel. / They are going to help.

Veja a diferença no mesmo contexto:

📞 "The phone is ringing!" → "I'll get it!" ← decisão agora

📅 "Who's calling the client today?" → "I'm going to call at 3pm." ← já estava planejado

🌦️ "It will be a great summer!" ← previsão/esperança

🌦️ "It 's going to rain — look at those clouds!" ← evidência
